A personal health intelligence system that connects daily nutrition to wearable data — and uses AI to make it effortless.

Where this came from

In March 2023, Pete — a software architect and database developer — was diagnosed with MGMT-methylated Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM), the most aggressive form of brain cancer.

After surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, Pete was left with something most patients aren't: a technical skillset, a research mindset, and a determination to take every possible measure to support his own recovery.

One of those measures was therapeutic ketosis — keeping daily carbohydrates below 50g to starve cancer cells of glucose. The evidence base for this in GBM is growing, and for Pete it became a non-negotiable part of his daily protocol.

But tracking it precisely, every day, while also monitoring weight, HRV, sleep quality, and activity from multiple wearables — and connecting all of that data meaningfully — was beyond what any existing app could do.

"I didn't want an app that tracked my steps. I wanted to know whether yesterday's calories went to fat or muscle. I wanted to know if my ketosis was holding. I wanted my data to work for me — not sit in someone else's cloud."

So Pete built MaxHealth. A single HTML file, running locally on his Android phone, with no account required, no subscription, and no data leaving his device. The AI handles meal logging. The pipeline handles wearable data. The dashboard connects it all.

He's been using it daily ever since. And now he's giving it away.

What it does

MaxHealth is two things that work together:

A nutrition tracker — log meals by typing, describing, or photographing them. The AI identifies macros instantly. A personal food library remembers your regular foods with locked values. At the end of every day, one tap copies a formatted row to your nutrition log.

A wearable data aggregator — import data from Withings, RingConn, Garmin, Oura, Apple Health, or any device that exports a CSV. The pipeline normalises everything into a single daily record. The dashboard plots weight trends, sleep quality, HRV, SpO2, steps, and activity — all in one place, all connected to your nutrition.

The question MaxHealth is designed to answer: what is my eating actually doing to my body?

How it works

  • 01Everything runs locally on your Android phone in Chrome. No server, no cloud, no account.
  • 02A single HTML file is the entire app. Download it, open it, use it.
  • 03AI meal logging uses your own API key — Claude or OpenAI. Your data never passes through MaxHealth.
  • 04Wearable data is processed by a Python pipeline on your device via Termux. Again — local only.
  • 05Everything is free. The code is open. You can read every line of it.

A note on privacy

Pete's diagnosis is mentioned here because it matters — it explains why MaxHealth was built the way it was, and why it might matter to you. But the app itself contains no reference to any individual's medical history. It is a tool. What you track in it is entirely your own.

No data is collected. No analytics. No advertising. No third party ever sees what you log.
