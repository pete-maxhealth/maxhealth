⚠ What changing these numbers actually does ▾
Calories set the pace, not the destination. Since protein and carbs stay fixed, moving your calorie/fat target changes how big your daily surplus is — that's what controls how fast you gain, not whether the weight is "good" weight. Roughly: ~250–400kcal/day surplus is a slow, controlled gain (~0.2–0.4kg/week); 500kcal+ is faster but increasingly likely to add more fat than lean tissue.

Fat % here is a ketosis requirement, not a body-composition lever. The high fat percentage exists to maintain therapeutic ketosis for the GBM protocol — it doesn't drive muscle growth. Protein plus resistance training does that. A ketogenic macro split isn't optimised for lean-mass gain the way a moderate-carb bulk would be; without a training stimulus, surplus calories default toward fat regardless of the macro split.

If you're logging Strength Training, that consistent stimulus is what shifts a given surplus toward more muscle and less fat — the macro numbers alone can't do that on their own.

If you're unsure, smaller changes are safer than bigger ones. A modest adjustment you can observe over a week or two beats a large jump you have to guess the effect of.

Carb counting method
UK labels are usually already total carbs. Some countries' labels separate out fibre, which many trackers subtract as "net carbs" since fibre is minimally glycemic. Defaults to Total — switching only affects today's ceiling check reliably; older logged days may not have fibre recorded, so this can't be safely applied retroactively.

Incidental Steps Baseline ▾ hide
If your TDEE was calculated assuming a "Moderate" or more active lifestyle, that figure already includes an assumed amount of daily incidental movement. To avoid double-counting, Daily Steps only credits extra calories for non-walk steps above this baseline — a sedentary TDEE assumption would mean a lower baseline (or 0), while Moderate/Active assumes more is already covered.
Lifestyle changed? Pick the level that matches your TDEE assumption now, not when you first set it:
Easy
~5,000
Moderate
~7,500
Hard
~9,500
steps/day
Easy = sedentary-to-lightly-active TDEE assumption (occasional movement, little structured exercise). Moderate = regular training plus reasonable daily movement — matches a "Moderate" TDEE multiplier. Hard = very active lifestyle, daily exercise plus an active job or lifestyle on top.

Add New Device / Data Source

Got a device not natively supported? Upload its CSV export and AI will map the columns automatically.

Requirements: CSV file, one row per day, must include a date column. Most health app exports work — Garmin Connect, Google Fit, Samsung Health, Apple Health (via export tools), Oura, WHOOP etc.

What happens:
1. Upload the CSV — AI inspects the headers and sample rows
2. Review the proposed column mapping
3. Confirm → config saved permanently
4. Future imports from this device use the saved mapping automatically

If the mapping looks wrong: tap Cancel and check your export settings — some apps export weekly summaries rather than daily rows. Daily granularity is required.
